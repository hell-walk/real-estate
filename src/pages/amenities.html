What is on
the fourteen acres

The club, the stream, the loop, the grove, and the things that make a Tuesday easier.

Ten acres of it is ground

The walking loop

1.4 km along the ridge edge, lit until eleven, with a 180 m stretch of basalt steps down to the stream and back. Four benches face west; the fifth faces the sunrise for the one person who gets up for it.

The club, 24,000 sq ft

A 25 m lap pool and a shallow one beside it, a gym of 2,400 sq ft that opens onto the lawn, two squash courts, a badminton hall, a library, a crèche, a 60 seat hall with a kitchen, and the terrace.

The stream and the grove

The monsoon stream runs from June to October; the 1974 mango grove of 38 trees stands beside it, with the children’s lawn in the bend. The harvest in May goes to whoever climbs.

Built from the hill

The boundary wall, the stream steps and the club plinth are dressed basalt from the foundation cut. The blocks are concrete frame with 230 mm fly ash brick, and every west wall is double skinned against the afternoon sun.

Water, power, and the gate

Rain tanks of 2.1 million litres under the lawns, two borewells, a sewage plant whose water goes back to the gardens, a 180 kW solar roof over the parking, full power backup for homes, and a gate manned around the clock with a second exit for the fire tender.

Everyday things

A convenience store and a chemist at the gate, a pick up point for the school buses, two guest rooms at the club bookable by residents, 1.5 parking bays a home underground, and 60 visitor bays above ground that nobody has to fight over.
